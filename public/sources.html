Sources

Every feed war-brief reads, with a short note on why. The brief scans these every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday morning and pulls items posted in the last three days.

Companies

Anduril

Defense tech company building autonomous systems and the Lattice software backbone. Founded 2017 by Palmer Luckey and others. Their blog announces product launches, contract wins, and occasional strategic essays from leadership.

Palantir

Data and AI platform company. Two products that matter for defense: Gotham (intelligence and military) and AIP (AI orchestration layer). Their blog mixes product news with applied case studies, often involving customers Anduril also sells into.

SpaceX

Launch, satellite communications (Starlink and Starshield), and increasingly a defense-relevant infrastructure company. The updates feed covers launches, mission successes, and major announcements.

Kratos

Mid-tier defense contractor. Makes the XQ-58A Valkyrie (reference design for the Collaborative Combat Aircraft program), target drones, and satellite ground systems. Plays a complementary role to Anduril: high-volume, lower unit cost, often as a teammate or subcontractor.

Amazon Project Kuiper

Amazon's low-Earth-orbit satellite constellation, the most credible challenger to Starlink and Starshield. Kuiper Government Solutions stood up specifically to win Department of War and intelligence community business. Worth tracking for whether the government treats it as a real second source.

Trade press

Breaking Defense

Daily defense industry coverage with strong access to program offices and primes. Best read on day-to-day acquisition news, budget moves, and program decisions. Reporters worth knowing: Sydney Freedberg, Theresa Hitchens, Lee Hudson.

Defense News

Sister to Breaking Defense in coverage scope but with different editorial emphasis. Stronger on service-specific reporting (Army, Navy, Air Force breakdowns) and international defense. Stephen Losey, Courtney Albon, and Joe Gould are the bylines to watch.

War on the Rocks

Long-form defense and strategy commentary. Run by Ryan Evans. Mix of academics, military officers, and policy practitioners writing essays rather than news. Slower cadence but heavier per piece. The place to read the conceptual debates that drive doctrine.

DefenseScoop

Technology-focused defense news. Sharper coverage of AI, autonomy, cyber, and software acquisition than the generalist trade outlets. Brandi Vincent and Jon Harper lead the reporting.

Acquisition reform analysts

Eric Lofgren — Acquisition Talk

Defense acquisition researcher and podcaster. Deepest open-source explainer of how the Pentagon actually buys things: budget structure, OTA mechanics, the politics of the colors of money. If you want to understand why a program is stuck, Lofgren has probably written about it.

Pete Modigliani — Defense Tech and Acquisition

Former MITRE acquisition fellow. Writes a weekly Substack on the defense industrial base and acquisition reform. Practical, pattern-focused, often surveys recent deals and contracts. Complements Lofgren: less wonky on process, more focused on the deal flow.

Naval and maritime

CDR Salamander

Retired Navy commander writing on national security, maritime strategy, and naval issues. Has 12,000+ subscribers and ranks high in Substack's News category. Sharp, opinionated, and the most accessible critic-from-within of how the Navy thinks about force design. Helpful counterweight to the air- and space-heavy slant of most defense tech coverage.

Think tanks (author-filtered)

The brief pulls from these institutional feeds but filters to specific writers, since the institutions publish too much for the brief to absorb everything.

AEI — American Enterprise Institute

Center-right policy think tank in Washington. Defense and foreign policy is one of its strongest areas. The brief filters to five writers:

  • Ross Douthat — New York Times columnist and AEI nonresident fellow. Reads as a cultural and civilizational analyst more than a defense specialist, but his work on American decline and renewal frames the same questions Anduril is trying to answer. Worth reading when he writes on China, statecraft, or the American future.
  • Hal Brands — Henry A. Kissinger Distinguished Professor at Johns Hopkins SAIS, AEI fellow. Strategist and historian, focused on great-power competition with China and the structure of American grand strategy. One of the most-cited voices in the current defense intellectual class.
  • Kori Schake — Director of foreign and defense policy studies at AEI. Former State Department policy planning, NSC, and Defense Department official. Writes on alliance management, civil-military relations, and U.S. defense policy.
  • Mackenzie Eaglen — AEI senior fellow on defense budget, force structure, and military readiness. The go-to analyst when you want to know what the Pentagon is actually buying and whether the budget supports the strategy.
  • Bill Greenwalt — Former Deputy Under Secretary of Defense for Industrial Policy. One of the architects of the modern OTA framework. Writes on acquisition reform, defense industrial base, and how to make the Pentagon buy faster.

Hoover Institution

Conservative-leaning policy think tank at Stanford. The brief filters to three:

  • Christian Brose — Anduril's Chief Strategy Officer, Hoover visiting fellow. Former staff director of the Senate Armed Services Committee under McCain. Author of The Kill Chain: Defending America in the Future of High-Tech Warfare, the book that articulated the autonomy-and-software thesis Anduril is built around. Read him to understand the strategic framing of the company you are about to join.
  • Jacquelyn Schneider — Hoover fellow, defense scholar focused on cyber, autonomy, drones, and emerging technology in war. Brings academic rigor to questions that are usually argued at the level of opinion.
  • Niall Ferguson — Historian, Hoover senior fellow. Writes on the long arc of American power and the historical parallels to today's great-power competition. Not a defense specialist, but provides the historical frame.

Think tanks (unfiltered)

CSIS — Center for Strategic and International Studies

Bipartisan think tank in Washington, founded 1962. The closest thing the defense policy world has to a default establishment voice. Heavy on briefs, analyses, and digital features across defense budget and acquisition, missile defense, space, and great-power competition. Names to watch include Seth Jones (defense), Tom Karako (missile defense), Mark Cancian (force structure), and the China Power team. Output volume is high, so the brief's job is to surface the few items most relevant to autonomous systems and acquisition reform.

SCSP — Special Competitive Studies Project

Bipartisan initiative founded in 2021 by Eric Schmidt to make recommendations for U.S. competitiveness in AI and emerging tech, building on the work of the National Security Commission on AI. CEO Ylli Bajraktari ran NSCAI before this. Singular focus on the U.S.-China techno-economic competition through 2030, with regular memos, podcasts, and the AI+ Expo. When SCSP publishes on autonomy, AI for national security, or industrial base, it usually matters.

Vandenberg Coalition

Non-partisan but Republican-aligned foreign policy network founded to promote a strong American foreign policy. Board chaired by Elliott Abrams. Network includes H.R. McMaster, Paula Dobriansky, and other former senior officials. Publishes policy reports, statements, and the "Flash Focus" briefings on breaking foreign policy news. Useful for tracking the conservative foreign policy establishment's current positions.

Investor and founder commentary

Trae Stephens (Medium)

Co-founder and Executive Chairman of Anduril, Partner at Founders Fund. Wrote the foundational pieces on why Silicon Valley should work with the Pentagon, why Anduril exists, and why the "business of war is the business of deterrence." His Medium has gone quiet — most posts are from 2021-2022 — but when he writes, it tells you where the leadership of the company you are joining is pointing. He also speaks frequently on podcasts and at conferences, often with more current commentary than his written archive shows.

Noah Smith — Noahpinion

Economics writer, former Bloomberg Opinion columnist, 447,000+ Substack subscribers. Posts roughly five times a week on economics, technology, geopolitics, and culture. Center-left, techno-optimist. Most posts will not be defense-relevant; the brief reads them anyway so it can catch the ones on industrial policy, the U.S.-China competition, manufacturing reshoring, and the political economy of the defense industrial base. Worth tolerating the off-topic items for the on-topic ones.

Cultural and philosophical

Andy Crouch

Writer and partner for theology and culture at Praxis. Author of The Tech-Wise Family, The Life We're Looking For, and Strong and Weak. His central frame for technology — that tools require and reward human involvement, while devices bypass and replace it — is the cleanest articulation of what makes Anduril's pitch different from a defense prime that ships a black box. Lattice is sold as a tool for the warfighter, not a device that replaces him. Crouch does not write about defense, but the frame matters every time he posts.

Federal data

SAM.gov

The U.S. government's official contract opportunities database. The brief scans five NAICS (North American Industry Classification System) codes for new solicitations:

  • 541715 — Research and development in physical, engineering, and life sciences
  • 334511 — Search, detection, navigation, guidance, aeronautical instruments
  • 336411 — Aircraft manufacturing
  • 336414 — Guided missile and space vehicle manufacturing
  • 541512 — Computer systems design services (often defense-relevant)

The synthesis filters these down further to the 2-3 items most relevant to Anduril and adjacent watchlist companies.
